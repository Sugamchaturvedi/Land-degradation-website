Understanding Land Degradation in India

Explore the mechanisms, spatial distribution, ecological drivers, and socio-economic ramifications of land degradation. Calibrated with datasets from the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD).

Explore Degradation Types Watch Educational Lectures

1. Water Erosion

Affecting 11.01% (36.19 Mha) of India's landmass, water erosion is the dominant physical process. It begins with raindrop splash detaching particles, advancing through sheet wash and rill incising into deep ravines and badlands, as seen across the Chambal valley.

Major States: Maharashtra, MP, Odisha, Karnataka

2. Wind Erosion & Desertification

Dominant in the hyper-arid and arid zones of Western Rajasthan and Gujarat (17.91 Mha). High-velocity summer winds detach dry sand grains via saltation and surface creep, engulfing roads, settlements, and agricultural canal banks in sand sheets.

Major States: Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana

3. Soil Salinity & Alkalinity

Affecting over 3.96 Mha, secondary salinization results from excessive canal flood irrigation without adequate sub-surface drainage. High capillary evaporation deposits toxic white crusts of sodium and chloride salts that destroy soil structure.

Major States: Gujarat, UP, Punjab, Haryana

4. Vegetation Degradation

The second largest driver in India (29.83 Mha), occurring when forest canopy cover is stripped by unscientific timber lopping, overgrazing by high livestock populations, or shortened shifting cultivation cycles (Jhum) across the northeast.

Major States: Jharkhand, Nagaland, Tripura, Assam

5. Waterlogging & Flooding

Affects 0.77 Mha where high monsoon water tables create anoxic root conditions. Flat topography in North Bihar and Eastern UP traps surface runoff in saucer-shaped chaurs, precluding Kharif crop cultivation.

Major States: Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal

6. Frost Shattering & Rocky Scree

Affects 4.41 Mha in the high-altitude Himalayas. Rapid freeze-thaw cycles fracture bedrock into unstable scree and glacial moraines, compounded by permafrost thaw in Ladakh and Himachal Pradesh.

Major States: Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between land degradation and desertification?

Land degradation is a broad term encompassing the decline in land productivity anywhere in the world. Desertification specifically refers to land degradation taking place in arid, semi-arid, and dry sub-humid areas (defined by an aridity index between 0.05 and 0.65) according to the UNCCD definition.

What is India's commitment under the Bonn Challenge?

India originally pledged to restore 21 million hectares of degraded land by 2030. At UNCCD COP14 in New Delhi in 2019, the Prime Minister announced an enhanced national target to bring 26 million hectares of degraded and deforested land into active restoration by 2030.

How do satellite sensors detect land degradation?

Sensors such as IRS AWiFS measure spectral reflectance across visible, near-infrared (NIR), and shortwave-infrared (SWIR) wavelengths. Indices like NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) track vegetative vigor, while thermal and shortwave bands detect surface salt encrustation, active gullies, and soil moisture stress.
